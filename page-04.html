PG 04 · The Autonomous Buyer · Contract Negotiation & Award

From 156 pages of supplier draft
to two executed contracts.
Every clause scored. Every redline explained.

The Contract Agent receives the rfx.awarded event from PG 03, loads both supplier draft contracts from Ariba Contract Workspace, extracts and classifies every clause using a rule-based NLP pipeline, scores each clause for risk with an XGBoost model whose SHAP output is written to Firestore before any redline is generated, and routes all high-risk clauses to Legal for review. No clause is autonomously accepted above the risk threshold. No contract is autonomously executed — ever.

Agent Architecture · Dual-Pipeline Design

Two contracts. One agent.
Shared HITL checkpoints.

The Contract Agent processes both supplier contracts in parallel. Track A (Medpack SE, 62 pages, standard GTC) and Track B (Sterimäk AG, 94 pages, heavily modified terms) converge at both HITL nodes — Legal reviews clauses from both contracts in a single session, CPO signs both in a single decision. Processing them separately would double HITL overhead and create negotiation timing asymmetry. See ADR-007.

ML Model Specifications · Two Models

Two models. Different jobs.
Both auditable. Both explainable.

Clause extraction and clause risk scoring are distinct problems requiring distinct architectures. The extractor is deterministic and classification-only — no SHAP required. The risk scorer is probabilistic and high-stakes — SHAP is mandatory before any HITL presentation.

Model A — Clause Risk Scorer
Model type
XGBoost classifier (v1.4) — trained on 6 years of ClaraVis contract negotiation outcomes. Retrained semi-annually via Vertex AI Pipelines. Deterministic output for identical inputs — required for reproducible audit under EU AI Act Article 13.
Input features
Eight features per clause, all derivable from the extracted clause text and ClaraVis standard playbook:
Clause type Deviation magnitude Jurisdiction flag Liability cap ratio Termination notice days IP assignment breadth CSRD coverage score Force majeure scope
Output
Risk score 0.0–1.0 per clause, plus 4-dimension breakdown: Financial · Operational · Compliance · IP. SHAP values for all high-risk clauses (≥0.75) written to Firestore before HITL_LEGAL is presented — EU AI Act Article 13 compliance.
Thresholds
≥ 0.75 — High Risk: HITL_LEGAL mandatory · agent cannot autonomously accept or redline
0.45–0.74 — Medium: agent redlines autonomously using ClaraVis standard playbook
< 0.45 — Low: agent accepts supplier clause or applies standard playbook substitution
SHAP contract
Top-2 risk dimensions per high-risk clause rendered as horizontal bars in the Legal HITL interface. Legal Counsel sees why a clause is high-risk — not just the score. This is the EU AI Act Article 13 transparency obligation expressed as a per-clause UX contract, not a post-hoc audit report.
Model B — Clause Extractor
Architecture
Rule-based NLP + ML classifier hybrid. Stage 1 (deterministic): rule engine segments both contract documents into clause units using section header patterns, numbered-list markers, and defined-term anchors. Stage 2 (learned): fine-tuned BERT classifier assigns each clause to one of 9 types. See ADR-006.
Clause types
Liability Termination IP Assignment Payment Terms Confidentiality Force Majeure Compliance / CSRD Warranty Governing Law
Explainability
Classification only — no risk decision, no SHAP required. BERT classifier confidence score written to audit log per clause. Stage 1 rule output is fully deterministic and human-readable: a compliance auditor can re-run it and get identical clause boundaries. This reproducibility is the reason the hybrid was selected over a pure LLM approach.
Model registry
Both models stored in Vertex AI Model Registry with versioned Model Cards. Every inference log entry references the model version hash — enabling point-in-time audit of any past clause classification or risk score.

HITL Specification · Two Checkpoints

Legal review. Then sign-off.
No contract executes without a human.

GDPR Article 22 prohibits automated decisions that produce significant legal effects. A contract execution is the definition of such a decision. HITL-2 is therefore unconditional — no risk score, no spend threshold, no clause count can trigger or bypass it.

HITL-1 — Legal Clause Review
Entry condition
Any clause risk score ≥ 0.75 (High Risk) OR any clause deviating from mandatory ClaraVis standard terms (CSRD Annex I disclosure, data protection minimum, liability cap floor)
Routed to
General Counsel / Legal (S-04) — named approver per legal governance policy. Deputy Counsel if primary unavailable beyond 8 hours.
Presentation contract
Original clause text · Agent redline · Risk dimension SHAP bars (top-2 dimensions) · Plain-English justification · ClaraVis playbook reference clause · Supplier track label (A — Medpack SE or B — Sterimäk AG)
Decision interface
Clause-by-clause: Approve redline / Modify redline inline / Accept supplier clause as-is (mandatory justification ≥50 characters). Each decision written to Firestore independently before advancing to the next clause.
Timeout behaviour
48 hours from routing → escalate to CPO + engage outside counsel. Agent remains paused. No autonomous redline on any timed-out high-risk clause — ever.
Audit record
{approver_id, clause_id, timestamp, decision, modified_text, justification, risk_score, shap_ref} → Firestore per clause (immutable append — IAM write-only, no update or delete permitted)
HITL-2 — Contract Sign-off
Entry condition
Always — unconditional. GDPR Article 22 prohibits automated execution of contracts that produce significant legal effects on ClaraVis or its suppliers. No risk score, spend threshold, or clause count overrides this gate under any circumstances.
Routed to
CPO (S-01) — single approver for both contracts in a single decision session. CFO (S-02) notified (not co-signature required) when combined contract value exceeds €3M.
Presentation contract
Final portfolio risk score delta (pre vs post negotiation) · Unresolved clause summary with Legal Counsel recommendation · CSRD Annex I status per supplier · Combined contract value · Supply continuity countdown (days to incumbent contract expiry)
Decision interface
Execute both contracts / Execute Track A only, return Track B to NEGOTIATE / Reject both — return to NEGOTIATE. On Execute: agent writes both contracts to Ariba Contract Workspace, activates both S/4HANA vendor masters, and fires contract.executed to Pub/Sub — all in a single atomic sequence.
Timeout behaviour
72 hours from routing → escalate to CPO + CFO with supply continuity risk flag (incumbent contract expiry countdown displayed). Agent remains paused. No autonomous execution under timeout.
Audit record
{approver_id, timestamp, decision, unresolved_clauses_acknowledged, contract_value_a, contract_value_b, model_version} → Firestore (immutable append)

Architecture Decision Records

Every design choice has a record.

Four ADRs govern this module. ADR-004 is carried from PG 03 — the same Firestore audit log pattern applies unchanged to every clause decision and HITL record in this module.

ADR-004 · Firestore for Audit Log
Immutable append — carried from PG 03, applies unchanged
GDPR Article 22 and EU AI Act Article 12 require a complete decision trail for every clause risk inference and every HITL decision. Firestore append-only IAM enforces immutability at infrastructure level — no application code can delete or modify a logged clause decision. Every HITL-1 per-clause decision and the HITL-2 sign-off record are written independently, ensuring a partial review can be audited even if the session is interrupted.
Status: Accepted
ADR-006 · Rule-Based NLP + ML Classifier over Pure LLM Extraction
Determinism and auditability in clause segmentation
A pure LLM extraction approach produces non-deterministic clause boundaries and classifications — the same document may segment differently across runs. For legal contract analysis, reproducibility is a compliance requirement: a compliance auditor must be able to re-run extraction and get identical clause boundaries and type assignments. The rule-based Stage 1 segmenter is deterministic by construction; Stage 2 BERT classification adds learned signal while maintaining auditability via confidence scores. Alternative rejected: pure LLM extraction (stochastic boundaries, non-reproducible output, no confidence calibration, fails EU AI Act Article 13).
Status: Accepted
ADR-007 · Dual-Pipeline with Shared HITL Checkpoints
Parallel contract tracks — one Legal review session, one CPO sign-off
Processing Medpack SE and Sterimäk AG contracts sequentially would double HITL overhead and create negotiation timing asymmetry — Sterimäk AG would receive redlines 48+ hours after Medpack SE, creating leverage imbalance between the two suppliers. Parallel processing with shared HITL sessions normalises the negotiation timeline and reduces total calendar time from 28 days to under 8. Alternative rejected: sequential processing (doubles HITL wait time, creates supplier leverage asymmetry, extends total cycle time beyond incumbent contract expiry window).
Status: Accepted
ADR-008 · GDPR Art. 22 as Unconditional HITL-2 Trigger
No spend threshold, no risk score — always human
GDPR Article 22 prohibits automated decisions that produce legal effects or significantly affect natural persons or legal entities. A contract execution creates binding legal obligations on ClaraVis and the supplier — it is categorically within Article 22's scope regardless of contract value or risk score. HITL-2 is therefore unconditional by legal necessity, not architectural preference. Implementing a score-gated bypass would constitute a GDPR violation regardless of the threshold chosen. Alternative rejected: score-gated HITL-2 allowing autonomous execution of low-risk contracts — rejected as non-compliant with GDPR Article 22, no acceptable threshold exists.
Status: Accepted
